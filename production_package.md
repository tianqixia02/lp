### SCENE 4 — HOW IT WORKS / AUTOMATION (1:05–1:40)

**Narration (V.O.):**
> "Step inside and it's a city unto itself. Giant ship-to-shore cranes — some taller than the Statue of Liberty — lift 40-ton containers off vessels in under a minute. But here's what's truly mind-blowing: almost nobody is there. Yangshan Phase IV is the world's largest fully automated container terminal. Laser-guided cranes lock onto containers with millimeter precision. Fleets of autonomous electric trucks shuttle loads without a driver in sight. And a central AI brain coordinates it all from a control room that looks straight out of a science fiction film — managing thousands of moves per hour, 24/7, 365 days a year."

**Visual Direction:**
- Dramatic low-angle shot of a towering STS crane arm sweeping overhead.
- Cut to close-up: spreader locks onto container corners — sparks of metal contact.
- Wide shot reveal: empty terminal floor — no humans, just rows of AGVs (Automated Guided Vehicles) moving in synchronized patterns.
- Interior shot of the AI control room: operators monitoring dozens of screens, digital twin of the port visualized in real-time.
- Split-screen comparison: LEFT = live camera feed of terminal, RIGHT = digital twin overlay showing AI decisions.
- Quick cuts of robotic yard cranes stacking containers in perfect rows.

### SCENE 5 — WHY IT MATTERS / GLOBAL SUPPLY CHAIN (1:40–2:10)

**Narration (V.O.):**
> "So why does the world's largest port matter to *you*? Because roughly 90 percent of everything you own spent time on a container ship. That smartphone in your pocket. The sneakers on your feet. The coffee you drank this morning. Chances are they all passed through Shanghai. When this port slows down — even by a little — the entire world feels it. In 2021, a single week of congestion here triggered shipping delays that lasted for months across Europe and North America. Shanghai isn't just moving boxes. It's moving the global economy."

**Visual Direction:**
- Montage sequence: quick close-ups of everyday objects — phone, shoes, coffee cup, laptop, headphones — each with a brief "shipping label" graphic overlay showing origin → Shanghai → destination.
- Pull back to reveal all objects sitting on a conveyor belt that feeds into a container.
- Cut to world map animation: red pulse radiates outward from Shanghai along shipping lanes to every continent.
- News-style footage of backed-up ports / empty store shelves (2021 supply chain crisis reference).
- Return to Shanghai port — ships moving, global flow continuing.
